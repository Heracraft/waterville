Who makes the rules that a Waterville code inspector enforces

Four groups write the rules: the city, the State of Maine, the courts and private code publishers. This page shows who writes what, how much legal force each document has, and where the inspector uses it.

Who is involved

Select a level to show fewer cards. Each card tells what that organization makes or decides.

Show

City of Waterville

City Council

Adopts and changes ordinances and fee schedules. Gives staff permission to take enforcement cases to court.

Makes: City Code chapters, new laws, attachments

Code Enforcement Office

Three staff: Director Dan Bradstreet, Code Enforcement Officer Adam Bradstreet and Ordinance Compliance Officer Todd Buckmore. The office issues permits, does inspections, answers questions and enforces the code.

Makes: permits, certificates of occupancy, notices of violation, forms

Planning Board

Reviews site plans, subdivisions and shoreland applications. The City Planner supports the board. The Code Director reports on approval conditions.

Decides: site plan, subdivision and shoreland approvals

Board of Zoning Appeals

Hears appeals of inspector decisions. An appeal must be filed within 30 days. The board makes a new decision on the full facts. The board also gives variances.

Decides: appeals, variances (§ 275-6.2)

Fire Department

Does the life safety review for building permits. Keeps the rental registry. Inspects for business licenses and short-term rentals.

Makes: life safety reviews, the rental registry

City Solicitor

The lawyer for the city. Handles court cases. Answers legal questions that the inspector cannot answer alone.

Makes: legal opinions, court filings

Electrical and plumbing inspectors

The Municipal Electrical Inspector checks work on one- and two-family houses (§ 127-9). The Local Plumbing Inspector issues plumbing and septic permits. The city does not publish who holds these positions.

Makes: electrical and plumbing permits, inspections

State of Maine

Maine Legislature

Writes the statutes in the Maine Revised Statutes (M.R.S.). Title 30-A sets the duties, powers and penalties of the inspector.

Makes: statutes

Maine Office of Community Affairs (MOCA)

Trains and certifies code enforcement officers. This program was in the Department of Economic and Community Development (DECD) before. MOCA publishes the building code rules, the certification rules and the training manuals.

Makes: MUBEC and certification rules, guidance manuals

State Fire Marshal

Enforces the Life Safety Code (NFPA 101) for the state. Issues construction and accessibility permits for many commercial and multifamily buildings.

Makes: state fire and accessibility permits

Department of Environmental Protection (DEP)

Writes the shoreland zoning rules. Each town ordinance must agree with these rules. Reviews work near protected natural resources.

Makes: 06-096 CMR ch. 1000, NRPA permits

Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS)

Writes the rule for septic systems. The rule controls septic design, the HHE-200 form and the septic work of the plumbing inspector.

Makes: 10-144 CMR ch. 241

Licensing boards

The plumbing and electrical licensing boards adopt the plumbing code and the National Electrical Code. Local inspectors apply these codes.

Makes: plumbing and electrical code rules

Courts

District Court

Hears land use citations under Rule 80K. This is how the city gets civil penalties. A certified inspector with permission can present the case for the city.

Decides: violations, penalties, orders

Superior Court

Hears appeals of decisions by the Board of Zoning Appeals and the Planning Board under Rule 80B.

Decides: appeals of board decisions

Maine Supreme Judicial Court

Writes the Maine Rules of Civil Procedure. Decides appeals. Its decisions apply to all towns.

Makes: court rules, case law

Publishers and vendors

International Code Council (ICC)

A private nonprofit organization. It writes the model building codes: IRC, IBC, IEBC, IECC and IMC. Officials, builders and engineers help write the codes, and a new edition comes every 3 years. Maine adopts the 2021 editions by rule. The ICC holds the copyright: the codes are free to read online, but copies and search cost money. The ICC also owns General Code.

Makes: model building codes

General Code (eCode360)

Shows the official City Code online at ecode360.com/WA3904. The ICC owns General Code since 2017. The eCode360 terms of use limit copies of the text.

Shows: the City Code online

NFPA

The National Fire Protection Association. It writes NFPA 101, the Life Safety Code, and NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code.

Makes: fire and electrical standards

IAPMO, ASHRAE, ASTM

IAPMO writes the Uniform Plumbing Code. ASHRAE writes standards for ventilation and energy (62.1, 62.2, 90.1). ASTM writes test standards, for example E1465 for radon.

Makes: standards that the codes refer to

Federal

FEMA

The Federal Emergency Management Agency. It makes the flood hazard maps and operates the National Flood Insurance Program. Chapter 145 (floodplains) of the City Code follows this program. The inspector is the city Floodplain Administrator.

Makes: flood insurance rate maps

Binding the inspector must apply it Procedure tells how to do the work Advisory explains; has no legal force

The largest gap. The model codes contain the technical rules that the inspector checks on site. Examples are stair dimensions, guard heights, fire separations and insulation values. The assistant can name these rules and give a link to the free ICC viewer. It cannot show the text. To show the text, the city must have a license from the ICC. We do not know if the city has one.

Editions also change. The city website shows the 2015 MUBEC and the 2017 NEC. The state adopted the 2021 ICC codes from April 7, 2025.
