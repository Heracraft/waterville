Who makes the rules a Waterville code inspector enforces

A building permit in Waterville draws on city ordinances, Maine statutes and rules, model codes written by private publishers, and state training manuals. This page maps who writes each one, how much legal weight it carries, and where it shows up in the inspector's day.

Order of authority

When two sources disagree, the higher one wins. A city ordinance may add local rules, but it cannot conflict with state law. A training manual explains the law and binds nobody.

State law
Maine statutes, passed by the Legislature. They set the inspector's powers, the penalty ranges and when the building code applies.
30-A M.R.S. § 4452, enforcement and civil penalties. 10 M.R.S. § 9724, the building code applies in towns over 4,000 people.
State rules
Agency rules, adopted under a statute. The building code rules adopt the ICC model codes by reference, which turns private books into Maine law.
MUBEC (16-642 CMR, now listed as 08-003 CMR), subsurface wastewater (10-144 CMR ch. 241), shoreland (06-096 CMR ch. 1000).
Adopted codes
Model codes and standards: the 2021 International Residential, Building, Existing Building, Energy and Mechanical Codes, NFPA 101, the plumbing and electrical codes.
Binding only in the edition and with the amendments the state rule names.
City law
The City Charter and the City Code, adopted by the City Council. Zoning, property maintenance, rental registration, floodplain and the local building code chapter.
§ 275-4.26 fences, § 205-7 property maintenance notices, § 127-3 permit fees.
Procedure
Court rules and city forms. Court rules govern how a case moves through court. Forms show what an applicant files.
M.R. Civ. P. 80K land use citations, 80B appeals. Building Permit Application, HHE-211 plumbing form.
Advisory
Guidance manuals from the state code enforcement training program. Useful for how-to, often years old, never law.
Legal Issues and Enforcement Techniques Manual (2017), Shoreland Zoning Manual (2008).

Court decisions sit beside this ladder. They interpret statutes and ordinances, and a Maine Supreme Judicial Court decision binds every town. Example: Mills v. Eliot, 2008 ME 134, on writing findings of fact for permit decisions.

Who is involved

Pick a level to narrow the list. Each card says what that body produces or decides.

Show

City of Waterville

City Council

Adopts and amends ordinances and fee schedules. Authorizes staff to take enforcement cases to court.

Produces: City Code chapters, new laws, attachments

Code Enforcement Office

Director Dan Bradstreet, Code Enforcement Officer Adam Bradstreet, Ordinance Compliance Officer Todd Buckmore. Issues permits, inspects, answers the public, enforces.

Produces: permits, certificates of occupancy, notices of violation, forms

Planning Board

Reviews site plans, subdivisions and shoreland applications. Staffed by the City Planner, with the Code Director reporting on conditions.

Decides: site plan, subdivision and shoreland approvals

Board of Zoning Appeals

Hears appeals of the inspector's decisions filed within 30 days, and hears them from scratch. Grants variances.

Decides: appeals, variances (§ 275-6.2)

Fire Department

Runs the life safety review on building permits, rental registration, business license and short-term rental inspections.

Produces: life safety reviews, the rental registry

City Solicitor

The city's lawyer. Handles court enforcement and answers legal questions the inspector cannot settle alone.

Produces: legal opinions, court filings

Electrical and plumbing inspectors

A Municipal Electrical Inspector for one- and two-family work (§ 127-9) and a Local Plumbing Inspector for plumbing and septic permits. Who holds each post is not published.

Produces: electrical and plumbing permits, inspections

State of Maine

Maine Legislature

Writes the statutes in the Maine Revised Statutes (M.R.S.), including the inspector's job, powers and penalties in Title 30-A.

Produces: statutes

Maine Office of Community Affairs

Runs code enforcement officer training and certification, which moved here from the old DECD program. Publishes the building code and certification rules and the training manuals.

Produces: MUBEC and certification rules, guidance manuals

State Fire Marshal

Enforces the Life Safety Code (NFPA 101) at the state level and issues construction and barrier-free permits for many commercial and multifamily projects.

Produces: state fire and accessibility permits

Department of Environmental Protection

Writes the shoreland zoning rules every town must match and reviews work near protected natural resources.

Produces: 06-096 CMR ch. 1000, NRPA permits

Department of Health and Human Services

Writes the subsurface wastewater rule that governs septic design, the HHE-200 form and the plumbing inspector's septic work.

Produces: 10-144 CMR ch. 241

Licensing boards

The plumbers' and electricians' examining boards adopt the plumbing code and the National Electrical Code that local inspectors apply.

Produces: plumbing and electrical code rules

Courts

District Court

Hears land use citations under Rule 80K, the inspector's route to civil penalties. An authorized, certified inspector may present the city's case.

Decides: violations, penalties, orders

Superior Court

Hears appeals from Board of Zoning Appeals and Planning Board decisions under Rule 80B.

Decides: appeals of board decisions

Maine Supreme Judicial Court

Writes the Maine Rules of Civil Procedure and decides appeals. Its decisions bind every town.

Produces: court rules, case law

Publishers and vendors

International Code Council (ICC)

A private nonprofit that writes the I-codes: IRC, IBC, IEBC, IECC, IMC. Maine adopts the 2021 editions by rule. The text is copyrighted; ICC offers a free read-only viewer and a paid Digital Codes Premium service.

Produces: model building codes

General Code (eCode360)

Hosts the official online City Code at ecode360.com/WA3904. Owned by ICC since 2017. Its terms of use restrict republishing the text.

Hosts: the City Code online

NFPA

Writes NFPA 101, the Life Safety Code, and NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code.

Produces: fire and electrical standards

IAPMO, ASHRAE, ASTM

IAPMO writes the Uniform Plumbing Code. ASHRAE writes ventilation and energy standards (62.1, 62.2, 90.1). ASTM writes test standards such as E1465 for radon.

Produces: referenced standards

Federal

FEMA

Maps flood hazard areas and runs the National Flood Insurance Program. Waterville's floodplain chapter (Ch. 145) and the inspector's role as Floodplain Administrator follow from it.

Produces: flood insurance rate maps

The gap that matters most. The model codes carry the technical rules an inspector checks in the field: stair geometry, guard heights, fire separations, insulation values. The assistant can name them and link the free ICC viewer, but it cannot quote them. Quoting would need a license from ICC, which the city may or may not hold.

Editions also drift. The city's web page still lists the 2015 MUBEC and 2017 NEC, while the state adopted the 2021 I-codes effective April 7, 2025.

The inspector's workflow

A permit runs from the first phone call to a certificate of occupancy. A complaint runs from a report to a notice of violation and, if nothing changes, to District Court. Each step lists the sources consulted, who else is involved, and which tool in the preview helps.

Intake

Phone and counter questions: do I need a permit, what can I build here, how far from the line. No online portal exists; applications are fillable PDFs.

Sources

Ch. 275 zoning use and dimension tables, Ch. 127 building, city forms

Who else

Residents, contractors, attorneys and title searchers asking about open permits

Preview: public answers with permit checklists, permit finder, fee estimate

Plan review and approvals check

Check zone, setbacks, lot coverage and the site plan. Decide which other approvals must come first.

Sources

MUBEC and the I-codes, NFPA 101, Ch. 275, Ch. 145 floodplain, Ch. 161 historic, Ch. 244 site plan, state rules

Who else

Planning Board, Fire Department life safety review, plumbing inspector, State Fire Marshal, DEP

Preview: project approvals checklist, research desk

Permit and fee

Issue and sign the permit. Work started without one pays double, capped at $500, rising to $1,000 after 30 days (§ 127-3B).

Sources

§ 127-3, fee schedules, the form's printed fees

Who else

Electrical and plumbing inspectors for their own permits

Preview: fee estimate from published fees only

Field inspections

At least three: foundation, framing before cover, final. A missed inspection call can cost $100 a day. This is where most of the week goes.

Sources

The I-codes and standards on site, mostly from memory or the ICC viewer

Who else

Builders, certified third-party inspectors (§ 127-5B)

Preview: offline re-reading of saved sections; the tool cannot inspect

Certificate of occupancy

Issued once the building meets MUBEC and the Life Safety Code (§ 127-6). Permit records are kept for the life of the structure.

Sources

§§ 127-5, 127-6, NFPA 101

Who else

Fire Department

Preview: case notebook for the property

Complaint

Property maintenance, junk vehicles, unsafe buildings. Residents call or email to book an appointment and fill out a paper form.

Sources

Ch. 205 property maintenance, § 210-10 vacate orders, 17 M.R.S. dangerous buildings

Who else

Fire Department, residents

Preview: printable complaint sheet, case notebook

Notice of violation

A written order citing the section, the facts, the fix, a deadline, the appeal right and the penalty range. Ch. 205 notices also go to the mortgagee.

Sources

§ 205-7, § 275-6.1, 30-A M.R.S. §§ 2691 and 4452, the Legal Issues Manual

Who else

City Solicitor for wording

Preview: notice drafter with Word export, deadline calculator

Consent agreement or court

Negotiate a fix, or file a Rule 80K land use citation in District Court. Owners are identified from registry deeds and an attested copy of the ordinance is attached.

Sources

M.R. Civ. P. 80K, the 80K Manual, 30-A M.R.S. § 4452(3) penalty tiers

Who else

City Council authorization, City Solicitor, District Court

Preview: court citation packet checklist

Appeal branch. Any inspector decision can go to the Board of Zoning Appeals within 30 days, heard from scratch. The board's decision can go to Superior Court under Rule 80B. The inspector serves as staff to the board: takes the $25 fee, schedules the hearing within 45 days, mails notice to owners within 300 feet at least 14 days ahead (§ 275-6.2).
