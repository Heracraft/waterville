# Chapter 61. Purchasing

*[HISTORY: Adopted by the City Council of the City of Waterville as last amended 8-9-2010 by Ord. No. 06-2010; amended 6-21-2022 and 10-1-2024 by Ord. No. 191-2024. Subsequent amendments noted where applicable.]*

### § 61-1. Purpose and policy.

As provided for in the City Charter, Article VII, Section 5, the purpose of this chapter is to provide procedures for the purchase or lease of equipment, supplies or services for the City of Waterville. The current policy of the City is to maintain a decentralized purchasing process which shall be monitored by the City Manager and Finance Director.

### § 61-2. Purchases or leases of $20,000 or less.

- A. Department directors are authorized to approve purchases of equipment, supplies or services of $20,000 or less in value, provided such purchases have been approved by the City Manager and are part of the department's annual budget appropriation. In addition, department directors are responsible for the following:

  - (1) To make every effort to secure the best possible price for all purchases or leases of $20,000 or less. This may involve an informal bidding process when, in the opinion of the City Manager or the department director, there are multiple vendors for that particular product or service.

  - (2) To plan future purchases to provide ample time to secure favorable prices.

  - (3) To assure that a sufficient unencumbered balance remains in the department's appropriation before a purchase is made.

  - (4) To advise and obtain approval from the City Manager for any purchase necessary for the continued operation of the department, which purchase may not have been approved in the annual appropriation or which purchase may exceed the department's total available funds.

- B. The City Manager shall ensure that the requirements of this chapter are not circumvented by multiple or separate purchases of $20,000 or less to avoid the bid requirements as outlined below.

### § 61-3. Purchases or leases in excess of $20,000.

- A. All purchases or leases in excess of $20,000 shall require a competitive bid process which is subject to review and approval by the City Council. Lease proposals with multiple-year payment terms shall indicate both the annual payment and total lease cost. This requirement shall not apply to the services of expert witnesses to be employed by the City in present or anticipated legal action.

- B. The following procedures shall be followed for all purchases and leases in excess of $20,000:

  - (1) In addition to notification to all known and interested vendors, the City shall publish a notice on the City's official website no later than five days before the bid deadline.

  - (2) The City Manager and/or the department director shall make every effort to ensure that at least three responsible bidders have been contacted for each purchase.

  - (3) All bids shall be submitted in a clearly marked, sealed envelope and opened in public at the hour stated in the notice.

  - (4) All original bids, together with all documents pertaining to the award of the contract, shall be retained and made a part of the permanent file or record and shall be open to public inspection upon request.

  - (5) All purchases and leases in excess of $20,000 require a purchase requisition and a copy of the signed resolution or order approved by Council prior to submittal to the Finance Office for payment.

  - (6) Items such as solid waste tipping fees, insurance costs, fuel purchase and other similar operational expenses approved by the Council as part of the annual budget process shall be exempt from the requirements of this section.

  - (7) If a purchase/project requires multiple payments, each invoice submitted for payment, after the original, must clearly reference the approved purchase order number and the approved Council resolution or order number or the invoice will be returned without payment.

### § 61-4. Bid review, approval or rejection.

- A. Contracts for the purchase or lease of equipment, supplies or services in excess of $20,000 shall be awarded only after authorization by the City Council. Written specifications for the purchase of such goods or services shall not be prepared so as to exclude all but one vendor.

- B. In addition to price, the Council may consider the following in determining the lowest responsible bidder:

  - (1) The ability, capacity and skill of the bidder to perform the contract or provide the service required;

  - (2) Whether the builder can perform the contract or provide the service promptly, or within the time specified, without delay or interference;

  - (3) The character, integrity, reputation, judgment, experience and efficiency of the bidder;

  - (4) The quality of performance of previous contracts or services as may be in part determined by contract with previous contractees;

  - (5) The previous and existing compliance by the bidder with laws and ordinances relating to the contract or service;

  - (6) The financial capability of the bidder to perform the contract or provide the service;

  - (7) The quality, availability and adaptability of the supplies or contractual services to the particular use required;

  - (8) The ability of the bidder to provide future maintenance and service for the use of the subject of the contract;

  - (9) The number and scope of conditions attached to the bid; and

  - (10) The energy efficiency of the product.

- C. For all purchases, the City reserves the right to accept and/or reject any and all bids or portions thereof.

### § 61-5. Waiver of bid process.

- A. Notwithstanding the foregoing requirements of § 61-4, the bid process may be waived under one or more of the following conditions:

  - (1) Price has been determined by a current and still valid bid process conducted by a recognized operating division of the state or county governments or the City Board of Education or a joint group composed of municipalities or quasi-municipal entities.

  - (2) When the Council is provided with factual data submitted by the administration that the price under consideration is the best available price and that, due to circumstances beyond the control of the administration, there is an insufficient time to resort to the normal bid process.

  - (3) When the Council is provided with factual data submitted by the administration that the matter under consideration is unique, noncompetitive or with a current vendor that has a contract with the City for specific goods/services.

  - (4) The Council may extend a contract for goods/services when it determines that it is in the best interest of the City to do so. Said extension shall be for no more than two years and will require a positive report from the administration that the contractor/vendor has met all contractual obligations and that there are important reasons to continue the relationship.

  - (5) When projects are entirely funded with privately raised funds.

- B. To waive the bid process, the City Council must determine by a two-thirds majority of the Council members present that Subsection A(1), (2), (3), (4) or (5) above exists.

### § 61-6. Procurement and purchasing with federal funds.

In the event that a federal award is used to procure or purchase property, goods and services, Addendum A shall be followed to ensure compliance with federal Uniform Guidance.[^1]
