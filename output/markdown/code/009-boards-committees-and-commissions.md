## Part 1. General Provisions

### Article I. Appointment and Operation

*[Adopted 4-19-2022 by Ord. No. 96-2022]*

#### § 9-1.1. Authority to appoint; residency requirement.

*[Amended 12-28-2023 by Ord. No. 199-2023]*

- A. In accordance with the authority granted in Article II, Section 5(G) of the Charter of the City of Waterville, the Mayor shall have the power to appoint members to boards, committees and commissions as those boards, committees and commissions are provided for by Charter, statute or ordinance.

- B. All mayoral appointees to boards, committees and commissions must be residents of the City of Waterville, unless otherwise specified.

#### § 9-1.2. Vacancy; procedure for filling.

*[Amended 5-20-2025 by Ord. No. 101-2025]*

- A. A mayoral appointment to a board, committee or commission shall be deemed vacant:

  - (1) If the member's term expires;

  - (2) If the appointee fails to qualify for the appointment by taking the required oath, when requested;

  - (3) By reason of death;

  - (4) By resignation;

  - (5) By removal and establishment of a primary residence outside of the geographic boundaries of the City;

  - (6) For absence of a member from more than 1/2 of the regular board, committee or commission meetings in a six-month period;

  - (7) For conviction of a Class A, B or C crime or a crime of any class involving moral turpitude;

  - (8) Violation of any of the duties of office.

- B. If one or more of the criteria set forth hereinabove to create a vacancy are met, the chair of the board, committee or commission shall inform the City Clerk of the condition that created the vacancy, and the City Clerk is to notify the Mayor of such a vacancy. The member of the board, committee or commission who is reported to have vacated the office is to be advised of this fact by the City Clerk at the time of the transmittal to the Mayor. If the appointee questions the accuracy of the facts that gave rise to the alleged vacancy, the appeal is directly to the Mayor, who shall be empowered to investigate further.

- C. Residents who are interested in being appointed must submit a completed application to the office of the Mayor.

- D. The Mayor must review all applications that have been submitted from residents interested in serving on any board, committee or commission and conduct interviews with each applicant, if necessary.

- E. The Mayor's recommendations must be approved by a majority of the City Council.

#### § 9-1.3. Appointment terms.

*[Amended 12-28-2023 by Ord. No. 199-2023]*

- A. All terms shall expire at the end of December in the year that the appointment term ends; however, members whose terms are expiring continue to serve until either they or their successor is appointed and qualified.

- B. All board, committee and commission members shall be appointed to term lengths of up to five years to establish and maintain staggered terms.

- C. Serving on a board, committee or commission does not guarantee reappointment when a serving member's term expires. Residents interested in being reappointed must submit a timely application and complete the interview process, if required.

#### § 9-1.4. Restrictions on authority.

*[Amended 5-20-2025 by Ord. No. 101-2025]*

Except for quasi-judicial boards, committees and commissions, boards, commissions and committees appointed by the Mayor shall not make promises or enter into verbal or written agreements on behalf of the City, unless authorized by the City Council.

#### § 9-1.5. Funding; support.

*[Amended 5-20-2025 by Ord. No. 101-2025]*

- A. Any funding assigned to a board, commission or committee by the Council shall be expended only through the administrative processes of the City under the direction of the City Manager.

- B. The City Manager shall assign a City employee to each board, committee and commission to serve as staff support.

#### § 9-1.6. Removing members of boards, committees and commissions.

If a board, committee or commission recommends removal of one of its members, the proposed removal shall be referred to the City Council for a decision, pursuant to Article V, Section 7 of the City Charter.

#### § 9-1.7. Oath of office.

All board, committee and commission members must swear an oath by a person authorized to administer oaths before being seated.

#### § 9-1.8. Ethics; conflicts of interest.

Board, committee and commission members shall be bound by Chapter 18, Code of Ethics, of the City Code.

### Article II. Ad Hoc Committees

*[Adopted 4-19-2022 by Ord. No. 97-2022]*

#### § 9-2.1. Purpose.

This article establishes the framework for ad hoc committees created to assist the City Council in the exercise of its powers, pursuant to Article IV, Section 7, of the City Charter.

#### § 9-2.2. Committee establishment.

*[Amended 12-28-2023 by Ord. No. 199-2023]*

Ad hoc committees will be created by resolution, which shall state, at a minimum, the following:

- A. Membership: the fixed number of members, including a Chair, to be chosen by the Council, and whether these members are to be Councilors, employees or other persons.

- B. Goals: the specific tasks the committee is expected to perform.

- C. Reporting date: the date by which the committee is to report back to the Council in writing with its recommendations or findings.

- D. Sunset date: the date by which the ad hoc committee terminates, which date can be extended by further resolution of the Council.

#### § 9-2.3. List of ad hoc committees.

*[Amended 2-21-2023 by Res. No. 45-2023; 12-28-2023 by Ord. No. 199-2023; 5-21-2024 by Ord. No. 100-2024; 2-3-2026 by Ord. No. 24-2026]*

- A. Organized with ongoing charge.

  - (1) City Manager Evaluation Committee (no sunset specified).

  - (2) Community Service Funding Advisory Committee (charge transferred to ARPA).

- B. Organized with finite charge.[^1]

  - (1) Parks and Recreation Advisory Committee (enacted 9-16-2025 by Res. No. 186-2025; sunsets 12/31/2027).

[^1]: Editor's Note: The following ad hoc committees with finite charges were formerly included in this section (sunset date): ARPA Committee (12/31/2023); Community Resilience Committee (12/31/2024); TIF Advisory Committee (12/31/2023); Transportation Committee (12/31/2023); Waterville Housing Committee (8/31/2024).

#### § 9-2.4. Addition or removal of ad hoc committee.

- A. Addition of ad hoc committee. When an ad hoc committee is added by Council action, it will automatically be added to the list of ad hoc committees in this article without further Council action.

- B. Removal of ad hoc committee. When an ad hoc committee is eliminated by either a sunset provision, or by express Council action, it will be automatically deleted from the list of ad hoc committees in this article without further Council action.

## Part 2. Specific Bodies

### Article VI. Comprehensive Plan Review Commission

*[Adopted 2-15-2022 by Ord. No. 47-2022; amended in its entirety 8-2-2022 by Ord. No. 165-2022]*

#### § 9-6.1. Commission.

*[Amended 5-20-2025 by Ord. No. 101-2025]*

- A. Establishment. There shall be a Comprehensive Plan Review Commission as required by Article XI, Section 9 of the City Charter.

- B. Composition. The Commission shall be comprised of 10 persons. Membership shall consist of one City Councilor, the City Manager, the Mayor and seven nonelected persons who need not be residents of the City and may be members of the Planning Board. Members shall be appointed by the Mayor with the approval of the City Council.

#### § 9-6.2. Convenings.

The initial convening of the Commission shall occur prior to June 30, 2022. Subsequent convenings shall occur at intervals of seven years thereafter. The City Council may convene a Commission when, in its judgment, circumstances warrant. Each convening shall terminate upon the Commission's delivery of its report to the Council.

#### § 9-6.3. Duties.

The Council shall adopt a charge to be given each Commission that sets out its role and the tasks it is charged with accomplishing, and such parameters of its authority and conduct as are common to other deliberative bodies established by the City Council. Each convening of a Commission shall simultaneously provide to the Planning Board and the City Council, within 24 months of its establishment, a report of its findings and recommendations in the form of a draft revised Comprehensive Plan with accompanying justifications.

#### § 9-6.4. Public input.

The Commission shall prepare the plan with public input and in accordance with 30-A M.R.S. § 4324 et seq., as amended from time to time, for adoption by the City Council.

### Article VII. Board of Assessment Review

*[Adopted 4-19-2022 by Ord. No. 98-2022]*

#### § 9-7.1. Jurisdiction.

- A. The Board of Assessment Review shall have jurisdiction to hear and decide appeals of taxpayers from the decision of either the Assessor or from the decision of the municipal officers in those cases where the municipal officers may act, on applications for an abatement of an assessment filed in accordance with 36 M.R.S. § 841, as that section may be amended from time to time. A taxpayer may apply in writing to the Board of Assessment Review within 60 days after notice in writing of the decision from which the appeal is being taken or after the application for abatement is deemed to have been denied in accordance with 36 M.R.S. § 842. A poverty abatement shall not be deemed denied. If the Board of Assessment Review determines that the taxpayer is overassessed, the Board shall have authority to grant such reasonable abatement as it thinks proper; otherwise, the Board will confirm the decision of the Assessor or the municipal officers.

- B. The Board of Assessment Review shall also have jurisdiction to hear Service Charge Appeals, pursuant to Chapter 5, Administrative Code, § 5-5.9.

#### § 9-7.2. Appointment.

The Board of Assessment Review shall consist of three permanent members and two associate members. All appointments to the Board of Assessment Review shall be by the Mayor with the approval of the City Council. The composition of the Board for the purpose of exercising jurisdiction shall consist of three members of permanent or associate status. An associate member may attend all hearings conducted by the Board of Assessment Review, but shall have no vote. If a permanent member of the Board is unable to vote due to inability to be present at a hearing or because of a conflict of interest, the Chair, or the Acting Chair, shall designate one of the two associate members to replace the absent or recused member, with full power to vote.

#### § 9-7.3. Procedures.

The procedures to be followed by the Board of Assessment Review shall be those procedures set forth in 30-A M.R.S. § 2691(3), as amended from time to time.
